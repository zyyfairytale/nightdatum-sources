Where does the water of AI data centres go?

Every number in the episode, in the order it is spoken: the source, the supporting passage or data, and for computed figures the arithmetic. Figures as of September 2026. Found an error? Open an issue on this site's GitHub repository — corrections are logged below.

“White plumes rising from the roof: up to one and a half litres of water gone into the sky for every kilowatt-hour of heat pushed out.”

1.482 L/kWh (heat rejected)
Theoretical water evaporation per kWh of heat rejected in a cooling tower, assuming 100% latent heat rejection at 30°C water temperature
Source: Derived: E/Q = 3600 * f_L / h_fg(T), with h_fg=2429.8 kJ/kg at 30C, f_L=1
derived (3600 kJ/kWh) / (2429.8 kJ/kg) = 1.482 L/kWh at f_L=1
Note: f_L=1 (all heat rejected as latent/evaporation) is a near-upper-bound simplification; real cooling towers reject 80-100% of heat as latent, remainder as sensible heat to air (per pdhonline.com/courses/m320 cross-check), so realistic range is roughly 1.19-1.49 L/kWh-heat at 30C.

“The wind carries it away, and whatever rain it becomes won't flow back to the local river or aquifer it was drawn from.”

'Consumed' water (USGS consumptive use) is the part of water withdrawn that is evaporated or otherwise removed from the immediate water environment, i.e. it is not returned to the source it was drawn from (that part is 'return flow')
Source: USGS Circular 1268, Estimated Use of Water in the United States in 2000 — Glossary
consumptive use — the part of water withdrawn that is evaporated, transpired, incorporated into products or crops, consumed by humans or livestock, or otherwise removed from the immediate water environment.
Note: Verified against the primary page (curl). Same wording in the USGS Water Science School glossary. LBNL's 66 bn L (f042) and ~800 bn L (f044) are consumption figures.

Record f070 · source dated 2026-09-25 · confidence high

“Every flip spends a little energy, and every electron pushed through the metal collides with atoms, shaking them faster.”

switching energy -> heat mechanism
Every time a CMOS gate switches, a tiny capacitance is charged or discharged; on a rising edge half the energy drawn from the supply is dissipated as heat in the pMOS transistor, and on the falling edge the stored energy is dumped to ground as heat in the nMOS transistor (dynamic power P = alpha*C*V^2*f)
Source: Harvey Mudd College, CMOS VLSI Design, Lecture 7 (Power)
Half the energy from VDD is dissipated in the pMOS transistor as heat, other half stored in capacitor ... Energy in capacitor is dumped to GND ... Dissipated as heat in the nMOS transistor.
Note: Retrieved via Perplexity; standard textbook result (Weste & Harris).
Record f067 · source dated 2026-09-25 · confidence high

“That shaking is heat.”

electron-lattice scattering -> heat mechanism
Joule heating at the microscopic level: electrons driven by the electric field repeatedly collide/scatter with the atoms (lattice vibrations, phonons) and hand them their energy, making the atoms vibrate faster, which is heat
Source: University of Texas at Austin, Energy in DC Circuits; Pop & Goodson (Stanford), Thermal phenomena in nanoscale transistors
Electrical energy is converted into heat (i.e., random motion of the atoms which make up the resistor) as the electrically accelerated free electrons inside the resistor collide with the atoms
Note: Second source: https://poplab.stanford.edu/pdfs/pop-goodson-itherm04.pdf

[Regulation text, Annexes I-III] requires reporting of total energy consumption (EDC), IT-equipment energy consumption (EIT), Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE = EDC/EIT), total water input (WIN) and potable water input (WIN-POT) in cubic metres, and Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE = WIN/EIT, EIT in MWh).
Note: Verified against locally fetched primary EUR-Lex text. Legal basis is the Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791, Articles 12 and 33(3). First reporting deadline was 15 September 2024, then 15 May 2025, annually thereafter. Applies to data centres with installed IT power demand >=500 kW. On 21 September 2026 the European Commission announced a further data-centre energy-efficiency package and is consulting on minimum-performance standards, with a legislative proposal planned for Q2 2027 - that is a DRAFT/future initiative, not yet adopted law; do not present it as already in force.
