“A line somewhere else is now carrying too much.”

Thermal and clearance limits; contingency power-flow redistribution mechanism
Transmission line current produces heat; heating and sag constrain safe loading, and loss of a line redistributes power to other lines
Source: Electricity Transmission: A Primer (National Council on Electricity Policy; hosted by DOE)
If, for example, a large power line drops out of service, the power flows will shift to other lines
Note: Physical and Technical Characteristics of Transmission, printed page 31, PDF page 40. Used only for stable physical mechanisms, not its historical description of regulatory authority. Hot-day outage scenario in the script is illustrative and is not a diagnosis of SJC37.

Record f027 · source dated 2025-10-15 · confidence high

“Because a large transformer is designed for a particular site.”

Electrical, thermal, and mechanical requirements are modeled for the site before fabrication design description
LPTs are custom-engineered to site-specific requirements
Source: Large Power Transformer Supply Chain Gap Analysis and Domestic Content Strategies for Hydropower Rehabilitation: Supplemental Report
The manufacturing process begins with extensive design work tailored to site-specific requirements necessitating detailed modeling of electrical, thermal, and mechanical stresses before fabrication can begin.
Note: The report date is May 2026; the exact date is from the OSTI catalog record. The quoted sentence describes site-specific design, not a particular numerical design value.
Record f082 · source dated 2026-05-06 · confidence high

“Even if the voltages match, its electrical characteristics may be wrong for the network receiving it.”

Same high- and low-side voltages do not guarantee interchangeability if impedance differs design description
Different impedance requirements can make otherwise similarly rated transformers non-interchangeable
Source: Transformer Resilience: Report to Congress
LPTs are very site-specific in their designs, so transformers with the same high- and low-side voltages but different impedances may not be interchangeable.
Note: The report explains why impedance and site-specific system needs matter; it does not state a numerical impedance value.
Record f084 · source dated 2024-07-10 · confidence high

“Utilities compete for limited production slots, specialised materials and skilled workers.”

116% power-transformer demand growth since 2019; constrained production slots demand growth and supply-chain mechanism
By August 2025, estimated US power-transformer demand had grown 116% since 2019; supply constraints increased competition for factory production slots
Source: Transformer troubles: manufacturing and policy constraints hit US transformer supply
increased competition for available production slots
Note: The article distinguishes 116% demand growth for power transformers from 274% for generation step-up transformers. It links production constraints to raw materials and specialist technical labour, as well as custom components. Rising lead time includes competition for available slots, not only fabrication duration. Checked 2026-10-08.

Record f106 · source dated 2025-11-10 · confidence high

“A facility could move some work to another site, run less equipment, or supply some electricity from its own generators.”

76 GW
New flexible load the US grid could accommodate at 0.25% curtailment
Source: Rethinking Load Growth: Assessing the Potential for Integration of Large Flexible Loads in US Power Systems
76 GW of new load—equivalent to 10% of the nation’s current aggregate peak demand—could be integrated with an average annual load curtailment rate of 0.25% (i.e., if new loads can be curtailed for 0.25% of their maximum uptime)
Note: Estimate covers 22 balancing authorities, representing 95% of US peak load. The report defines curtailment rate by annual curtailed energy relative to the new load’s maximum potential annual consumption; it is not simply a count of hours.
Record f056 · source dated 2025-02-11 · confidence high

1500 MW

Record f113 · source dated 2025-01-08 · confidence high

Many gigawatts GW of load
NERC identified simultaneous loss of very large loads as a potential reliability risk
Source: Characteristics and Risks of Emerging Large Loads
